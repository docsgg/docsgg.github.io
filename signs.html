Signs: Stop Signs

How to identify regions by their stop signs. Mostly standard, but with crucial exceptions.

🛑 The Standard Stop Sign

In most countries around the world, you will see the classic red octagon with white letters. This is the default; if you see it, you can usually rule out the countries listed in the exceptions below.

❗ Exceptions

These countries use a different design or have specific rules that break the global standard.

1. Japan

Uses a red inverted triangle with white Japanese text. However, you can sometimes find the standard road signs in some areas.

2. South Korea

Also uses a red inverted triangle, but the text and layout differ from Japan.

3. Thailand

Standard red octagon, but the text is in Thai instead of English.

4. Malaysia

Similar red octagon, but with Malay text ("BERHENTI").

5. North American Spanish Speaking Countries

Mexico, Guatemala, etc. Often use a red octagon, but with "ALTO" instead of "STOP".

6. South American Countries

Many South American countries (like Brazil, Argentina, Chile) use "PARE" or "PARADA" instead of "STOP".

7. Papua New Guinea

Uses a standard red octagon with "STOP" written in English, but the sign itself is a different shape (often a flattened or elongated version).

8. Nepal

The sign features the word "रोक्नुहोस्" (Roknuhos) in Devanagari script.

9. Israel, Ethiopia and Pakistan

These countries use alternative designs, often with local languages or different shapes (like a circle with a red border).
